# English release review

**Recommendation: not ready for release as a fully adaptive, comprehensive KS2 English programme.** A supervised supplementary-activity pilot is a more defensible scope once missing task materials and accessibility barriers identified by the wider review are resolved. Human specialist sign-off remains pending.

Reviewed 26 September 2026, app source version 0.1.0. Reviewer: AI-assisted English content review; no teaching qualification, classroom experience or professional accreditation is claimed. No children were contacted or observed. This is a source-level educational review, not a native-device, screen-reader or pupil-performance test. Sinéad Hoben’s approval of the educational approach remains separate from independent task approval.

## Scope and evidence

Read every prompt, passage, keyed answer, distractor, hint, explanation and checklist in **27 English trails / 81 tasks**: four English core trails in `src/content.ts` and all 23 trails in `src/english.ts`. Also reviewed the adult-rating and adaptation implementation, coverage mappings, handover and existing testing record. No unambiguous wrong keyed English answer was identified. This does not mean the questions supply sufficient teaching, assessment or progression evidence.

The complete register is [english-tasks.json](english-tasks.json), one row for each zero-based activity/level key, with displayed level, finding, correction and pending human approval. All 81 rows were reviewed; none are implicitly treated as signed off. [english-coverage.json](english-coverage.json) independently assesses every one of the 41 English coverage rows. These are **partial coverage**, because relevant task prompts exist but the supplied instructional sequence, text range, repeated practice or evidence is insufficient for a comprehensive curriculum claim. Partial does not mean every individual activity is defective.

The reference was the live official [CCEA KS2 Language and Literacy minimum-content document](https://ccea.org.uk/document/2054), retrieved on the review date, pages 1–2. Its requirements span oral language, reading and writing, including supported experiences, varied resources and purposeful composition. The audit does not substitute England’s curriculum or a transfer-test syllabus. The document is not a ready-made app progression or mastery rubric; mapping an activity to it does not validate difficulty levels. A qualified NI curriculum lead must confirm applicability at release.

Provide a way to leave work unassessed when materials/support are unavailable. | P2: Required source materials are absent or vague | Open `layout:1`: transform “a short paragraph” but none is supplied or requested from an adult (`src/english.ts:66`). `instructions:2` requires a shared grid town but provides none (`:47`). | Children/adults must invent the input before attempting the learning goal. | P2: Wording ambiguities and unsupported explanation | `instructions:0` says put a pencil beside a book and a rubber “above it” (`:45`); adult guidance notices ambiguity but child prompt does not make resolving it the objective. `source-detective:1` explanation asserts an observation method although the statement supplies only a count/date (`:14`). `talk:0` says read once while its hint permits repeats (`src/content.ts:38`). | Ambiguous task completion and a small overclaim about evidence quality can confuse learners and adults. | Clarify the pronoun or explicitly teach clarification; distinguish a recorded count from a documented method; permit repeat listening in the main instruction. | P2: Revision recognition is mapped to actual reconsideration | `detective:2` shows the full text and asks why Orla changes her mind (`src/content.ts:28`, ER10). | Recognising the character’s inference is not evidence that the child revised their own earlier interpretation. | Reveal text in stages; record an initial prediction and a revised view with evidence. Acceptance: review can distinguish retained, refined and changed child interpretations. |

Acceptance: lack of access does not produce a needs-support ability rating; alternatives preserve or clearly qualify the assessed goal. |

## Curriculum depth and progression

There is useful breadth: original short fiction, poetry and information text; inference; source evaluation; imaginative/factual/persuasive writing; purposeful discussion; and respectful attention to dialect. The app avoids timed performance pressure, claims that artistic quality equals reading comprehension, and automatic grading of creative writing. These are sound starting decisions.

However, three tasks per broad trail form a catalogue rather than a teaching sequence. The shortest tasks are reasonable access points for some P5–P7 learners, but their difficulty labels are not calibrated. Text complexity, independence, vocabulary, conceptual demand and group-resource demands vary together. Adult-selected texts make two children’s “same level” work incomparable. The unit tests check software keys and metadata, not these educational properties.

The 41-row matrix names the relevant activity mappings and exact limitations. Priority gaps within otherwise present topics are explicit guided reading; modelled/shared/guided writing; a substantial text library; actual child-supported evidence justification and revised interpretation; performed rather than named skimming/scanning; varied spelling/grammar application; and sustained evidence of personal style and handwriting fluency. External practical work can legitimately contribute, but must be supplied, supported and observed before counting it as demonstrated coverage.

## Practical backlog and re-review

1. **NI KS2 English specialist:** review the 81-row register, correct missing inputs/ambiguities and add task-specific criteria. Record named human qualifications, date, version and individual approvals. Acceptance: every task can be followed, all alternative valid responses are respected, and no task is marked approved merely because its key is correct.
2. **English specialist with assessment expertise:** construct subskill progressions and several equivalent items per step, including rich reading and extended writing. Work with the product reviewer on anti-replay and evidence rules. Acceptance: representative struggling, uneven and secure journeys have educationally justified next steps.
3. **Inclusion specialist and artistic director:** co-design text/audio/visual scaffolds, partner mode, short preparation cards and legible writing/layout tools. Acceptance: review pupils can understand what to do and access appropriate response methods without support availability being mistaken for attainment.
4. **Owner and qualified reviewers:** run consented, supervised trials across P5–P7 and different support needs; evaluate comprehension, task appeal, fatigue and adult-rating agreement. Record actual observations separately from expectations. Re-review changed items and claims against the final release build.

No product changes, publication, external contact or human sign-off occurred during this review.
